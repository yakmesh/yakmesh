Understanding the Dashboard

Learn what each metric means and how to interpret health indicators

Beginner ⏱️ 8 min read 📊 Node Monitoring

What You'll Learn

The YAKMESH dashboard is your window into how your node is performing on the mesh network. This tutorial explains every section of the dashboard in plain English, so you'll know exactly what's happening with your node at a glance.

  • Understand the Node Status section
  • Read Peer Connection metrics
  • Interpret Time Sync information
  • Monitor Content Storage usage
  • Recognize health indicators (green/yellow/red)

🖥️ Accessing the Dashboard

There are two ways to open your dashboard:

Method 1: Web Browser

Open your browser and go to:

http://localhost:8080

(The port may differ if you configured a custom port)

Method 2: Command Line

# Open dashboard in default browser
yakmesh dashboard

# Or get a text-based summary
yakmesh status --detailed

1 Node Status Section

This is the most important section - it tells you if your node is working properly. You'll typically see it at the top of your dashboard.

Node ID

Node ID: doko-node-qubit-lattice-pq-a7x9

What it means: This is your unique DOKO identity on the mesh network - a human-readable identifier derived from your cryptographic keys. The format is doko---. It never changes unless you create a new identity.

Online Status

Online Uptime: 3d 14h 22m

What it means: Shows whether your node is connected to the mesh network and how long it's been running. The uptime resets when you restart YAKMESH.

Online - Your node is connected and working normally
Connecting - Your node is starting up or reconnecting
Offline - Your node can't reach the network (check your internet)

Version

Version: 0.9.4 (latest: 0.9.4) ✓

What it means: The YAKMESH software version running on your node. A checkmark means you're up to date. If there's a newer version available, you'll see an update notification.

2 Peer Connections Section

This section shows how well your node is connected to other nodes in the mesh network. More connections generally means better performance and reliability.

Connected Peers

What it means: The number of other YAKMESH nodes your computer is directly connected to. Your node automatically maintains connections - you don't need to manage this manually.

● 40+ peers: Excellent connectivity - your node is well-integrated

● 10-39 peers: Adequate - works fine but could be better

● Less than 10: Low connectivity - may experience slower transfers

3 Time Sync (MANI) Section

MANI is YAKMESH's time synchronization system. Accurate time is critical for security and ensuring all nodes agree on when events happened.

Sync Status

Synchronized
Drift: +2ms

What it means: Whether your node's clock is in sync with the mesh network's consensus time. The "drift" value shows how much your clock differs from network time.

● Drift under 50ms: Excellent - your clock is accurate

● Drift 50-500ms: Acceptable - still works but consider syncing your system clock

● Drift over 500ms: Problem - some operations may fail; sync your system clock

Time Sources

📡 Mesh ConsensusActive (47 peers)
🌐 NTP FallbackStandby
⏰ Local ClockReference

What it means: YAKMESH uses multiple sources to determine accurate time. The mesh consensus is the primary method - your node asks many peers what time they think it is and calculates the truth.

ℹ️ Why time matters: Accurate time prevents replay attacks (someone resending old messages), ensures correct ordering of events, and is required for cryptographic operations. If time sync fails, some features may be temporarily disabled for security.

What it means: How much disk space YAKMESH is using for stored content. This includes files you're sharing and any content you've chosen to help host for the network.

  • Your Shares: Files and folders you're actively sharing with others
  • Received Content: Files you've downloaded from other nodes

5 Additional Metrics

Your dashboard may show additional information depending on what features you're using.

KARMA Score

Your Karma Score 847
Top 15% of network

What it means: Your reputation on the mesh network. Higher karma means you're a reliable participant - you keep your node online, help host content, and complete transfers successfully. Some features may require a minimum karma score.

NAMCHE Gateway Status

NAT Traversal: Full Cone
Direct connections available

What it means: NAMCHE helps your node be reachable even behind routers and firewalls. "Full Cone" is ideal - it means others can connect to you directly. "Symmetric NAT" is harder but still works.

SHERPA Services

Discovery Service Running
Relay Service Disabled
Bootstrap Service Disabled

What it means: SHERPA services you might be running to help other nodes. Discovery helps others find peers, Relay assists nodes behind difficult NATs, and Bootstrap helps new nodes join.

🚦 Understanding Health Indicators

Throughout the dashboard, you'll see colored indicators. Here's what each color means:

Green - Healthy

Everything is working as expected. No action needed.

Yellow - Warning

Something needs attention but isn't critical. Your node still works, but performance might be affected. Common causes: low peer count, time drift, storage almost full.

Red - Critical

A serious issue needs immediate attention. Some features may not work. Common causes: offline, severe time desync, disk full, authentication errors.

💡 Tip: Click on any yellow or red indicator to see suggested fixes. Most issues resolve automatically or have simple solutions.

⌨️ Command Line Dashboard

If you prefer the terminal, you can get all this information via commands: 

# Quick status overview
yakmesh status

# Detailed status with all sections
yakmesh status --detailed

# Just peer information
yakmesh peers

# Just storage information
yakmesh doko status

# Time sync status
yakmesh mani status

# Watch live updates (refreshes every 2 seconds)
yakmesh status --watch

Example output from yakmesh status:

🎉 Summary

You now understand all the major sections of the YAKMESH dashboard:

  • Node Status: Is your node running and up to date?
  • Peer Connections: How well are you connected to the mesh?
  • Time Sync: Is your clock accurate for secure operations?
  • Content Storage: What are you sharing and storing?
  • Health Indicators: Green = good, Yellow = check it, Red = fix it

Check your dashboard occasionally to ensure your node is healthy. Most of the time, everything runs automatically - but knowing how to read these metrics helps you troubleshoot if something goes wrong.
